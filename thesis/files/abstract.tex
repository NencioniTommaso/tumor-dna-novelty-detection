\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

{\itshape
Liquid biopsy is an emerging diagnostic approach based on the key idea that cell-free DNA fragments (cfDNA) retain the distinctive genetic and epigenetic traits of tumor tissue.
This technique is minimally invasive, as it only requires a simple blood sample from the tested subject, thus making it a promising alternative to traditional tissue biopsy, at least for an initial screening. 

This thesis investigates a novelty detection approach based on string kernels
applied to short DNA reads. We represent each read in a high-dimensional feature
space induced by the \emph{mismatch string kernel}, which counts all $k$-mers
within a specified Hamming distance of the observed subsequences. An extended
six-letter epigenetic alphabet --- incorporating 5-methylcytosine (M) and
5-hydroxymethylcytosine (H) alongside the canonical bases --- enables the kernel
to capture methylation signatures that are known to be altered in cancer.
In particular a One-Class Support Vector Machine (OCSVM) is trained only on sequences originating from healthy subjects, in order to define a decision boundary that might be able to separate healthy sequences from cancer sequences, thus providing a preliminary indication of tumor presence based on the degree of similarity with the healthy population. 

While this work \textbf{does not aim to provide definitive clinical diagnoses}, it investigates the viability of string kernels and One-Class SVMs for epigenetic novelty detection. Ultimately, a high anomaly score generated by this system could serve as a preliminary flag, alerting clinicians to the potential presence of a tumor and prompting further targeted investigation.
}
